# Breadsheet: A scheduling tool for homemade bread and pizza

## How is this different from a recipe?
[Breadsheet](https://breadsheet.com) adds a handy scheduling tool to your recipes, helping you fit long recipes into your day.

Most bread recipes take considerably longer than your average dinner recipe. Often an _order of magnitude_ longer, and sometimes with hours of downtime between steps. That downtime can vary wildly depending on ambient temperature, humidity, etc.

Bread recipes identify windows of time when the dough displays certain properties. You then perform an action, then leave the dough to rest until the next window arrives. And since some of these recipes are measured in _days_ (I'm looking at you, sourdough), planning your life around these steps can be challenging.

Breadsheet shows you those ranges for the start (or finish) time specified, allowing you to play with those timings to fit that recipe into your schedule today. 

## Got an example?
Suppose you're invited to a picnic on Saturday afternoon. Captivated by your exemplary breadmaking skills over the years, the group asks you to bake a fresh loaf of sourdough.

Although you love sourdough, you're also aware it can be challenging to properly schedule. There are many time-sensitive steps in this process with a fair bit of downtime throughout. You may need to start as early as Thursday(!) depending how your [levain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sourdough#Starter) is stored. And if you don't already have a mature levain, you aren't making sourdough this week. 

But wait, this is `${DateJoke.getFullYear()}`!  There _must_ be a better way.

Enter [breadsheet](https://breadsheet.com): a scheduling tool for long bread recipes that calculates the start & finish times for the recipe itself, and each step along the way.

Focus on the bread, and leave the scheduling to breadsheet.
